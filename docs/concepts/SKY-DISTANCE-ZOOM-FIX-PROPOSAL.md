# Mother Earth — Sky distance zoom fix

**14 November 2026 · rev 2 · proposal for Chad**

## What needs fixing

Chad's brief is “zoom that stays inside the box.” This proposal applies it to the Sky scene on Earth now. The Earth, + Moon and + Sun stops should stay instant, and zoom should keep the scene usable within the globe panel.

GitHub main was verified at [49c8c7e](https://github.com/TheOneTheOnlyTheChad/mother-earth/commit/49c8c7e), the docs-only close-out after the Sun globe release at e50a033. The handover records hub 0.6.14.

**Live reproduction, desktop Chrome, 14 November.** Open Earth now → Show the two clocks → Zoom out to the Moon and Sun → + Sun. At 1470 × 801, with a 902 × 614 canvas, the scene reported the 14 November 00:00 UTC sample, Moon 405,584 km and Sun 148.0 million km. At + Sun, an upward wheel gesture over the canvas changed the projected rings while the page scroll position stayed at zero. Drag near the middle of the canvas to enter free orbit, then wheel up repeatedly in the same area. The canvas went blank and all three labels disappeared, while the stop bar and distance strip remained. Re-frame restored all three labels and selected + Sun. The + Moon stop showed Earth and Moon. Real touch pinch, Safari trackpad pinch and screen-reader operation still need checks. [Public scene](https://theonetheonlythechad.github.io/mother-earth/)

**Claude’s independent reproduction on main 49c8c7e.** Headless Chrome with the committed catalogue reproduced zoom-in going blank and found a second failure on zoom-out. In free orbit the camera reached 200,000 globe units from its target while the far plane was 125,000 units. The meshes disappeared while the SVG rings, tags and lines remained. Re-frame recovered both cases. See the companion `CLAUDE-REPRO-2026-11-14.md`.

**Source diagnosis.** In `skyscene.js`, guided zoom clamps its path parameter from Earth to + Sun. The intermediate path checks Earth's framing. After a drag, the free-orbit branch instead multiplies the camera offset along its current line of sight. The camera controls' distance limits are broad and the branch does no viewport containment check or forward-depth check against the far plane. A distance bound alone can still leave a camera aimed away from the bodies. The overlay is already clipped to the panel, so this needs a camera fix.

## Proposed behaviour

| Action | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Earth, + Moon or + Sun button | Select the stop immediately. Earth is the anchor at Earth, Earth and Moon are framed at + Moon, and all three are framed at + Sun. |
| Zoom in or out | Follow the existing continuous Earth ↔ + Moon ↔ + Sun path and stop at either endpoint. Provide visible Zoom in and Zoom out buttons with keyboard access. |
| Drag, then zoom | Keep free orbit for looking around. On the first zoom input in either direction, return to the last guided position, apply that increment and clamp to the path. Apply this to every supported zoom input, even when the increment points past the current endpoint. Re-frame still jumps to the corresponding named stop. This makes the first zoom after a drag a predictable reorientation. |
| Trackpad or touch pinch | A pinch beginning in the canvas moves the Sky camera. A pinch elsewhere follows normal browser behaviour. Handle cancellation and finger changes without a stuck gesture or an unintended drag. |
| Plain wheel | Keep ordinary scrolling where the layout allows it. Use Ctrl+wheel over the canvas for camera zoom, including trackpads that emit it for pinch. Zoom buttons provide the mouse and keyboard path. This proposed change to the original Sky wheel interaction matches the Moon globe convention and is a scope choice for Chad. |
| Resize, rotate the device or change overlay height | Recompute the usable canvas and guided framing when dimensions change. Keep the current guided progress and use the existing explicit unavailable state when the scene cannot fit. Closing the scene still restores the Earth view. |

On the guided path, keep Earth visible throughout. A close Earth may fill the panel. At the named stops, render the applicable bodies and keep their rings and tags clear of the controls and distance strip. Along the guided path, attach body tags only where the corresponding mesh is drawable within the camera frustum, including its near and far planes. Keep any intentional occlusion treatment distinct from clipping. Between stops, the Moon or Sun may move into or out of view as the camera travels. Free orbit can also temporarily obscure bodies. Its recovery control must stay visible, and a zoom from there should recover framing in the same input.

Preserve the pinned Horizons sample, computed timestamp, imagery date, actual distance labels and existing disclosure about the Sun being drawn closer and resized. Keep the Explore links tied to the displayed timestamp. Preserve the saved stop and link focus on return, and restore the original globe camera, layers, controls, and focus on exit. Page-side scope with zero incremental running cost.
