## 2. What the leads show about the slice

1. **"Five or six relatives, both ends, open primary sources" does not hold up.** Only Homo naledi has both ends in one open source, and that range is one cave deposit. Every other open lead gives one end, one site or one region.
2. **The ends are different kinds of thing.** A deposit's age bracket (naledi), a minimum age with no maximum (Omo I), a layer's age (the Denisovan rib), a modelled regional disappearance (Neanderthals in France and northern Spain). The catalogue already has matching date shapes (`interval`, `bound`, `interval_modelled`); a reader must be able to tell them apart.
3. **The earliest fossil is not the origin, and the latest is not the extinction.** An open review found in passing (Bobe & Wood, "Estimating origination times from the early hominin fossil record", CC BY-NC-ND according to its first page as shown in a search extract) is about exactly this. No entry may say "appeared" or "died out" on the strength of a first or last dated fossil.
4. **Tool evidence is not fossil evidence.** Higham 2014 and the Liang Bua tool range date artefacts attributed to a group. They stay out of a first slice, or are a separately labelled claim.
5. **Several well-known dates are behind paywalls** (Ngandong, Liang Bua, the Europe-wide Neanderthal date, Jebel Irhoud, Drimolen's dating). The six Big Five sources are all CC BY 4.0 and were all read in full.
