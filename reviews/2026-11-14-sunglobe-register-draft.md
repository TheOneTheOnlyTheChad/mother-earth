## NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory — HMI continuum "latest" browse image `latest_1024_HMIIC.jpg` — **hourly hub collection enabled 14 Nov 2026; shown as the Sun globe on `sun.html` once released**
- **Filed:** <date and time> on Chad's line "<Approve line>".
- **Source:** NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory, https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/ . File `https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/assets/img/latest/latest_1024_HMIIC.jpg` — Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager continuum intensity, colourised by SDO, 1024 × 1024 JPEG, about 200 KB. SDO "Best Practice for getting SDO Browse Data", https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/bestpractice.php (read directly: GPT 14 Nov 2026; Claude, browser pane, 14 Nov 2026 09:55 UTC): near-real-time images "updated every 15 minutes", "designed for Space Weather operators and citizen scientists"; "Best practice would be to retrieve a new images once every fifteen minutes"; the address pattern above.
- **Request method:** **scheduled** — the hub (Cloudflare Worker) makes one request an hour, with no retry, when `SUNFACE_ENABLED=1` (set 14 Nov 2026 06:44:21 UTC; first manual run 06:38 UTC; first scheduled run 07:07 UTC). User-Agent sent: `mother-earth-collector/<version> (+https://github.com/TheOneTheOnlyTheChad/mother-earth; contact: issues at that address)`; whether SDO's server received it is not verified. The page never calls SDO. SDO's own example fetches every 15 minutes; we ask a quarter as often. No stated limit on automated access was found on the pages read.
- **Reuse terms — SDO "Data Rights and Rules for Data Use", https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/rules.php (read directly: GPT 14 Nov 2026; Claude, browser pane, 14 Nov 2026 09:54 UTC):** "SDO images and movies are not copyrighted unless explicitly noted. The use of SDO images for non-commercial purposes and public education and information efforts is strongly encouraged and requires no expressed authorization. It is requested, however, that any such use properly attributes the source of the images or data as: 'Courtesy of NASA/SDO and the AIA, EVE, and HMI science teams.'" NASA's general "Images and Media Usage Guidelines" (https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/, read directly 13 Nov 2026) cover informational web pages and computer graphical simulations, ask that NASA be acknowledged, and forbid implied endorsement.
- **SDO's Rules of the Road on the same page (science use), recorded because they sit beside the image terms:** "Browse products are not intended for science analysis or publication and should not be used for those purposes without consent of the PI." And: "Users are also encouraged to make available value-added data products. Users producing such products must notify the PI and must clearly label the product as being different from the original PI-produced data product."
- **Our use, stated specifically:** a credited **public display** of one browse image, for general information, **reprojected onto a sphere and masked**, and labelled as such on the page ("wrapped on the globe by us"; what is not drawn; the times). It is not science analysis and nothing is published from it as a measurement. We do not offer it as a data product. **The PI has not been notified.** Whether a display of this kind is a "value-added data product" under the Rules of the Road is our reading that it is not; if Chad prefers certainty, a short note to SDO is his to send.
- **Credit asked, and shown:** "Courtesy of NASA/SDO and the AIA, EVE, and HMI science teams." — on `sun.html` (Sources and method) and in `sources.html`.
- **What we keep and show:** the bytes unchanged in our storage (`sunface/images/<id>/sun.jpg`). **Only the current and the previous picture are served. Older picture files are still retained in storage** (about 200 KB an hour; no cleanup built), with one small marker per picture ever published. This differs from the proposal (rev 2.3 said only current and previous are kept) and is recorded here as a deviation **accepted by Chad for this release / to be corrected** (delete as decided). Three times kept apart: the file's Last-Modified ("File updated at …", shown), the hub's retrieval time (shown), and the observation time (**not known; never shown**). A picture is displayed for at most three hours after its file-updated time.
- **Our modifications (display only):** wrapped on a sphere by orthographic projection from the disc centre and radius measured in the picture and the solar tilt B0 computed for the date (Meeus); the limb band beyond 0.97 of the radius, the sky and SDO's printed caption are not drawn; the rest of the sphere is drawn plain and labelled "outside this image's view".
- **Placement check (ours, one frame; the evidence used for release):** 14 Nov 2026, picture `20261114T070005Z-4bc25d7a146de6fd` against NOAA SWPC Solar Region Summary No. 318 (issued 14 Nov 00:30 UTC; positions valid 13 Nov 24:00 UTC; GPT verified the nine source coordinates and the validity time against the original). Six of the nine regions were matched **within about 1° of latitude and 4.5° of longitude**, after allowing for rotation over an assumed 6.5–7 hours. The comparison uses whole-degree region coordinates, individual spots, and an assumed observation window. It supports north up and east to the left **for the checked image**. The SDO pages read give no north-up guarantee for this feed, and no automated alignment test exists. A one-off manual read of an SWPC text product already registered; not a run-time source.
- **Not used:** SDO's other channels and sizes; the dated browse files; the synoptic maps.
- **No endorsement implied.** No NASA or SDO logo is shown; SDO's printed caption is masked.
- **Commercial use:** SDO's page speaks of non-commercial and public-information use; commercial use is not addressed there. Mother Earth makes none.
