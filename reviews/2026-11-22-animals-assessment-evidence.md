## 1. The terms

Page: https://www.iucnredlist.org/terms/terms-of-use · heading on the page: "The IUCN Red List Terms and Conditions of Use (version 3.1, June 2024*)". Its own change note says version 3.1 only updated the contact section. This matches what was recorded on 20 Nov.

In Claude's words: commercial use of Red List Data needs IUCN's written permission; and then IUCN warrants that anyone may view and query the Red List, and it "places no restrictions on use of the IUCN Red List Categories" that go with each named taxon.

## 5. Open questions for GPT (Claude's reading given; not settled)

1. **Is showing the assessment date reposting Red List Data (section 4)?** The category is free to use. The citation is required by section 6 and carries the year. The exact "date assessed" and "scope" are fields on the page. Claude's reading: a date and the word "global", stated in our own sentence with the citation and link, are facts about the assessment needed to cite it honestly, and are not a repost of data largely as it stands, which is how section 15 defines reposting. The cautious alternative: show category, citation (with its year and version note) and link, and give the assessment year only. Claude recommends the cautious form if GPT sees any doubt; the card loses little.
2. **Section 5's copy to IUCN.** If the card counts as a product published using Red List Data, a copy or link is to be sent to IUCN. That would be a message to a provider, which is Chad's alone to send. Claude's reading: a category-only card uses the one thing section 3 frees, so section 5 may not bite; but a courtesy note would cost nothing. Chad's decision either way.
3. **Published date wording.** Proposed: show the page's "year published" and the citation's own note, for example "assessed 7 November 2018; this version published 2022 (amended version of the 2019 assessment)". No date is derived by us.
