## EUMETSAT — "GeoColour RGB - MTG - 0 degree" (collection `EO:EUM:DAT:0913`), pictures from the EUMETView map service, layer `mtg_fd:rgb_geocolour` — Earth from Space — **filed before any picture is stored; hub not collecting; not on the page** (branch `earth-from-space-rc`)

- **Filed:** <date> on Chad's line "<his line>". Text per `docs/concepts/EARTH-FROM-SPACE-STORAGE-TO-RELEASE-PROPOSAL.md` rev 2.2 section 7 and the source proposal `docs/concepts/EARTH-FROM-SPACE-PROPOSAL.md` rev 2 (branch `earth-from-space-proposal`).
- **Source:** EUMETSAT. Catalogue entry https://user.eumetsat.int/catalogue/EO:EUM:DAT:0913 (read 19 Nov 2026). Its "Explore in EUMETView" link goes to the `mtg_fd:rgb_geocolour` layer; its Access tab lists EUMETView only. The WMS layer's own metadata carries no licence or catalogue link, so the match between layer and catalogue entry rests on the catalogue's link, as for Dust RGB.
- **Licence (catalogue entry, "Data policy", verbatim):** "Free and unrestricted - CC-BY-4.0". Licence text: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
- **Credit asked for by the catalogue entry (verbatim):** "When GeoColour imagery and animation are used outside of EUMETView, both EUMETSAT and NASA should be given credit."
- **Read by Claude:** the catalogue entry (19 Nov 2026); for Dust RGB, EUMETSAT's "Data registration and licensing" guide V2.2 of 4 Nov 2026 (12 Nov), which says Core data and products are "made available without charge under a CC-BY-4.0 licence".
- **Not read by Claude or Chad:** the EUMETSAT Data Policy PDF (January 2026); any condition on the NASA Black Marble background beyond credit. GPT's reading of the Data Policy recorded in the Dust RGB entry concerned that product; **nobody has stated that reading for GeoColour**, and this entry does not claim it.
- **What the picture is (catalogue):** by day, True Colour RGB from the visible channels; by night, infrared cloud information, partly transparent, over the NASA Black Marble, which "is a static layer, and it doesn't reflect the actual conditions of the city lights". An observed satellite picture with a fixed night background, not a photograph of one instant.
- **Latency:** frames have been listed about 20 to 25 minutes after their time (one night's log, 19 Nov; morning and afternoon not yet sampled), so a frame can be under an hour old when it is read. As for Dust RGB, this rests on the product being offered under CC BY 4.0 by its catalogue entry, not on EUMETSAT's one-hour rule for Level 1 image data.
- **Credit as shown (in the record the hub serves, and on the page and in Sources when released):** "Contains modified EUMETSAT Meteosat data" with the year, the product name "GeoColour RGB - MTG - 0 degree", "CC BY 4.0", links to the catalogue entry and the licence; "Night-time background: NASA Black Marble (a static layer; not current city lights)". No EUMETSAT or NASA logo. Sources says neither endorses Mother Earth.
- **What we change ("modified"):** we ask the service for this projection (the satellite's own view, `EPSG:900003`), this size (2,048 × 2,048) and this surround colour. Pixels inside the disk are not altered. No numerical data is used or redistributed; only the rendered picture.
- **Requests:** hub only. One listing request (GetCapabilities for the `mtg_fd` namespace) and, when a newer frame is listed, one JPEG 2,048 × 2,048 (about 0.55 to 0.68 MB) and one PNG 256 × 256 (about 0.12 to 0.13 MB) for exactly that frame time. No retry inside a run. The page never contacts EUMETSAT. **Stage C: one manual run (3 requests; 6 at most). Stage D, if approved: one run every ten minutes for 288 slots, at most 3 requests a slot.** Before this entry: about 210 requests by the Stage A probe (20 to 21 Nov; its plan was 70 runs of 3) and 9 by Stage B (21 Nov), nothing stored. Service headers seen for Dust RGB: a rate limit of 20 (window not stated) and 8 concurrent. No published usage policy for the WMS was found. **Whether sustained requests warrant a note to EUMETSAT is Chad's open question (D3); no enquiry has been sent.**
- **A frame is published only if** both responses carry a `Warning` header naming the requested frame time, the delivered JPEG decodes in full in the hub, and it agrees with the check image. Otherwise nothing is published and no requested time is shown as a frame time.
- **Storage and serving:** the hub's R2 bucket under `earthview/`: the current and previous pictures and every picture that left those roles less than 24 hours ago (about 146 pictures, 80 to 100 MB, in healthy steady state under scheduled collection; one picture after Stage C). The hub serves the record at `/snapshot/earthview/meta` and each held picture at its own address. **From the first stored frame these are publicly downloadable by URL**, whether or not a page links to them.
- **Frame time:** what instant of the scan the frame time marks is not stated by the service and is not known to us. The page says so.
- **Data status:** observed satellite imagery, shown as a picture. Known limits stated in Sources: faint local damage in a late scan of a progressive JPEG may pass the checks; the night-side lights are not current.
- **Open:** D3 above; the Data Policy PDF unread for this product; morning and afternoon listing-delay samples; validation limits rest on few frames.
- **Commercial use:** CC BY 4.0 permits it with attribution; Mother Earth makes none.
