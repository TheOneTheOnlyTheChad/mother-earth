## 7. Known weaknesses, for GPT's judgement

1. **Landing view (538.8 Ma).** All four finds sit within 0.07 % of the right edge. Their marks are half cut by the lane's edge (`screens/landing-1280.png`). The captions and links are complete and each opens its entry, but the drawing is poor. Options: keep each mark's glyph inside the lane at this zoom (a nudge of up to 7 px, about 3 Ma here, on a mark already not to scale), or draw no marks on this lane above some window and say "too recent to draw at this scale" with the "Last 400,000 years" button. I have not chosen.
2. **Lane height.** The four captioned rows are 212 px tall at 1280 and 308 px at 390 (244 and 356 px when two rows carry "Bring into view"), plus the lane's heading and sentence, in every view, including the landing view.
3. **Phone, 0.05 Ma.** The La Ferrassie range is a 6.4 px bar. The link is 44 px, so it can be tapped; whether a 6 px bar reads as a range is a visual call.
4. **Focus visibility, screen-reader speech and a real phone** were not checked. Roles, names, focus targets and history were read from the page.
5. **The first tick label** at the left edge looks slightly cut in the 400,000-year screens. I have not checked whether main's page does the same at its own windows.
6. **`MIN_SPAN` and the thousand-year ticks apply only when the catalogue has finds.** The released page behaves exactly as before (ui I, last two checks).

## 8. La Ferrassie: what was read today, and what was not

PDFs are now allowed for private reading. The journal's own site still does not load here. A HAL record exists (`hal-04125428`, CC BY-NC-SA); my request to open it went unanswered at the screen, so **the full text is still unread**.

Read at abstract level only, from the journal's article page text and two repository records (Guérin et al. 2023, PaleoAnthropology 2023:1, 98–118, doi 10.48738/2023.iss1.811):
- The paper dates a sediment block associated with La Ferrassie 1 by OSL and models the ages of LF1, LF2 and LF8 with OxCal and with BayLum/ArchaeoPhases.
- With the OSL ages in the BayLum model, it places all three individuals in the late Middle Palaeolithic (younger than 52 ka at 95 % credibility) and says they could have been contemporaries at 44.9 to 44.1 ka.
- A search extract of the body says one Neandertal bone identified by mitochondrial DNA was dated among the radiocarbon samples, followed by a sentence beginning that something about it is not currently demonstrated. I could not read the rest.

What this means for the rehearsal entry: it dates **the bone fragment** (ETH-99102), not the LF8 individual, and already states the link to LF8 as the 2020 authors' inference. The 2023 paper gives a **modelled age for the individual** that is older than the fragment's calibrated range. That is a real difference a reader should not be left to trip over. Until the full text is read, the entry stays unfrozen, and GPT's option to release the other three without it stands. Enquiry E4 already asks the authors this, neutrally.
