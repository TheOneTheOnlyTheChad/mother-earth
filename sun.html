Parker Solar Probe

loading…

Mission on this page

  • NASA’s Parker Solar Probe follows a long, narrow orbit around the Sun, diving through the Sun’s outer atmosphere about every 88 days to sample it directly. Other solar observatories watch from Earth orbit or from near an Earth–Sun balance point; this page shows Parker only.

    Mission status: no separate status statement was checked. The trajectory source says its solution is “Fit to post-launch tracking data through 2026-Sep-29”. Source: NASA/JPL Horizons data sheet for Parker Solar Probe, revised 3 Nov 2026; checked 13 Nov 2026.

Sources and method

  • Both the dated example (22–27 December 2024) and the position for a current time: NASA/JPL Horizons, Sun-centred positions in the ICRF frame. The example is sampled every 10 minutes; the current read is hourly, fetched once a day by the Mother Earth hub. This page never contacts Horizons. Horizons
  • Trajectory description: taken from the Horizons data sheet returned in the same answer as the positions. December 2024 lies inside what Horizons calls its fit to tracking data; dates after 29 September 2026 are on what it calls a reference planning trajectory.
  • Closest approach: the figure given is NASA’s, from its own report. The “closest sample” is the nearest of this page’s 10-minute points and is not the exact minimum.
  • Cross-product check: compared with NASA/GSFC SPDF’s hourly product PSP_HELIO1HR_POSITION (DOI 10.48322/ecnj-rx61; data provided by N. E. Papitashvili and CDAWeb, accessed 13 Nov 2026). Distance from the Sun agrees within that product’s rounding; longitude around the Sun differs by up to about 0.85° near closest approach. The cause is not established, and the two are not assumed to be independent.
  • Sun radius: 695,700 km (NASA/JPL Horizons, “Solar radius (IAU2015)”). Heights above the surface are our subtraction.
  • Our changes: positions rotated by us to a view from above the Sun’s north pole, and rounded to 1 km.
