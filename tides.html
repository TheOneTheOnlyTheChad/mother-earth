How the Moon and Sun shape the tides

Computed Idealised tide model · Bulges exaggerated · Body sizes and distances not to scale · Not a tide prediction

The pale blue surface is a model: an ocean covering the whole Earth, land included, pulled only by the Moon and the Sun. Move the Moon and watch the shape change. It is not measured water and says nothing about any coast.

Moon at 0° from the Sun’s direction · new Moon · high-to-low difference 100% of the model’s largest

Moon and Sun in line (new Moon). The two stretches lie along the same line and add up: the largest difference between the model’s high and low. These are called spring tides. The name has nothing to do with the season, and new and full Moon give the same shape.

How this works
  1. Why two bulges. The Moon’s pull gets weaker with distance. It is stronger on the side of Earth facing the Moon than at Earth’s centre, and weaker still on the far side. Earth as a whole is being pulled too, so what matters is the pull at each place compared with the pull at the centre. Compared that way, the near side is drawn toward the Moon and the far side is left behind: both are stretched away from the centre. Nothing pushes the far side outward.
  2. The Sun does it too. The Sun pulls on Earth far harder than the Moon does, but tides come from how much the pull changes across Earth, and the Sun is about 390 times farther away. In this model its stretch is about 46% of the Moon’s.
  3. In line: spring tides. At new Moon and at full Moon the two stretches lie along the same line and add. The model gives exactly the same shape for both. “Spring” here has nothing to do with the season.
  4. At right angles: neap tides. At the quarter Moons the Sun’s stretch lies across the Moon’s. The difference between high and low in the orbit plane falls to about 37% of its largest. It does not fall to nothing, and the surface is not a sphere.
  5. Real oceans are different. Continents, depth, the shapes of ocean basins and the weather change real water levels and their timing. High water at a real coast need not come when the Moon is overhead, and not every place has two similar high waters a day. This model shows none of that.

What is left out on purpose: the tilt of Earth’s axis and of the Moon’s orbit (here the equator lies in the orbit plane), the changing distances of the Moon and the Sun, the time the ocean takes to respond, friction, the give of the solid Earth, and the ocean’s own gravity. Earth does not turn in this picture and the Sun’s direction is held still. Playback speed means nothing.

Sources and method

The model

  • Rule: value in any direction = P2(angle to the Moon) + 0.46 × P2(angle to the Sun), where P2(x) = (3x² − 1) / 2 and x is the cosine of the angle. The two parts are added before anything is drawn. A body that is switched off counts as zero. Model version tides-model-R1.
  • Where it comes from: the tide-generating potential in R. H. Stewart, Introduction to Physical Oceanography, §17.4 “Theory of Ocean Tides” (LibreTexts copy, read in full 25 November 2026), which also gives the Sun-to-Moon ratio 0.46051. The step from that potential to a surface shape is our own short derivation for a rigid Earth, constant gravity and a small, still displacement (surface = an equipotential, so height = −potential ÷ gravity). It is not a quotation.
  • Drawing: surface radius = 1.15 + 0.12 × value, in Earth radii, the same in every mode and at every angle (from 1.062 to 1.325 Earth radii, always outside the globe). The shape is greatly exaggerated. The model gives a relative shape only: no heights in metres are claimed, and water volume is not conserved by the drawing.
  • Scale: the Moon’s size against Earth’s is to scale. The distances and the Sun’s size are not, and they are never used in the calculation.
  • Light: Earth and the Moon are lit from the Sun’s direction with parallel rays. The Sun is drawn at its picture’s own brightness.

The pictures: reference images, not live

Moving the Moon does not change these pictures. They show what the bodies look like; they are not part of the calculation.
